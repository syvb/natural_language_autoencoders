Interpretability · Qwen3.6-27B NLA · single-case walkthrough

Two interpreters read one thought.
Both hallucinate the cult for the rite — their critics disagree about it.

A natural-language autoencoder (NLA) turns a model's internal activation into a written explanation, and a paired critic scores each line by how well it reconstructs that activation. This is one held-out case, start to finish — the smallest complete version of the comparison.

1

The activation: one token inside a bibliography

Both NLAs read the same Qwen3.6-27B activation, captured at layer 42 at the last token of this passage (a Hungarian sociology-of-science reference list):

…Previous publications in epistemology/sociology of science - Katona, Eszter, Németh, Renáta, Kmetty, Zoltán: Text analytics… - Bárdits, Anna, Németh, Renáta (2017): The rite of statistical significance◂ token 245

True next words (the model had not seen them): “ testing – contemporary critics; the rite in sociology.” — the real paper is “The rite of statistical significance testing.”

2

Both explanations invent the same famous book

Each NLA emits a list of short lines describing the activation. Both land on “The cult of statistical significance” — the real 2008 Ziliak & McCloskey book — a very natural slip from rite. An LLM judge (nex-n2-mini), shown the source, flags both as unfaithful.

◆ Matryoshka NLA · item 3

“Second citation “The cult of statistical significance” is a famous open-access article on statistical practice norms.”

CONTRADICTED marginal FVE −0.21

■ Standard NLA · matched sentence

“…“The cult of statistical significance” is an incomplete publication title, continuing the bibliographic list…”

CONTRADICTED marginal FVE +1.08
3

What “marginal FVE” means

Feed the explanation to the critic one item at a time and watch the reconstruction quality (fraction of variance explained). An item's marginal is how much it moves that number:

marginal(item k) = FVE(items 1…k) − FVE(items 1…k−1)

Negative marginal = adding the item made the critic's reconstruction worse. That is the signal I mined: the matryoshka's item is one of 272 top-3 items across 149 / 250 contexts whose marginal dips below zero.

4

Same fabricated claim, opposite verdict from the two critics

This is the whole finding in one picture. The identical “cult” claim hurts the matryoshka critic and helps the standard critic:

Deleting the matryoshka item actually raises its reconstruction by 0.12 FVE (leave-one-out) — the critic wants it gone. The standard critic treats the very same fabrication as load-bearing. (Standard marginals also ride high because its cumulative curve climbs out of a deeply negative start; the order-free solo & leave-one-out view in the aggregate figures tells the same story.)

What this one case shows — and what it doesn't

Across all 272 mined items, this pattern holds: 85% of the matched standard sentences carry positive marginal FVE (mean +0.42), and on order-free measures they reconstruct well alone and are load-bearing where the matryoshka item is redundant or harmful. The standard critic reconstructs from a sentence's surface form largely regardless of whether it's true; the matryoshka critic at least sometimes pushes an off-base claim down to zero or negative.

Honest caveat: a negative marginal is a weak hallucination detector, not a clean one. Most hallucinated items — in both models — still have positive marginal FVE, and the association isn't significant for the matryoshka (odds ratio 1.18, p = 0.19). This case is illustrative, not a lie-detector.
