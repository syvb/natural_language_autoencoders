Interpretability demo · two ways to translate a model's hidden state

Cross out an AI's thought — see what survives

Mid-sentence, a language model's internal state at one position is a list of 5,120 numbers. A translator network turns those numbers into English, and a second network reads only the English and tries to rebuild the numbers. The rebuild score is the test: it measures how much of the original state the description really carries.

We trained two translators. One writes a ranked list of short, independent notes — most important first (a “matryoshka” NLA). The other writes a normal paragraph. They score about equally when you read the whole description — but they behave very differently when you don't.

Reading on a budget

Suppose you only have time to read the first few words of each description (say, an automated safety monitor scanning millions of moments). Averaged over all 5,274 described moments: the ranked list gives you most of its value in the first line. A half-read paragraph is worse than reading nothing — the rebuilder is thrown off by the dangling prose.

It isn't just this example

0.7% vs 19.6%
How often deleting a unit that carried no measurable information still destroys a big chunk (>5 points) of the rebuild — across every unit of every described moment. The paragraph's rebuilder falls apart when the prose is disturbed; the ranked list barely notices.
65% vs 31%
Fraction of units you can delete essentially for free (<1 point of recovery lost). Independent notes cover for each other; every sentence of the paragraph is direly needed — including, often, ones that said nothing new.

Both translators explain Qwen3.6-27B layer-42 states and score ~identically when their full descriptions are used (recovery 0.50 vs 0.53 on average; 0.77 vs 0.79 on the moment above). “Recovery” is the fraction of variance of the state explained by the rebuild (FVE); 0% = knowing nothing, negative = misleading. Cross-out values are precomputed leave-one-out measurements over 5,274 positions (23,573 paragraph sentences, 52,526 list lines). Models: ceselder/nla-qwen36-27b-matryoshka and ceselder/qwen3.6-27b-nla-L42; the moment shown is from an agentic-scenario transcript at the token “independently”.
